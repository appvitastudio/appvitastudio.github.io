Why ZWJ and ZWNJ are sometimes needed

👨‍👩‍👧 is three emoji glued with two zero-width joiners: remove them and you get three separate faces. In Persian, the non-joiner separates parts of a word without a space (می‌خواهم). In Hindi or Malayalam the joiner selects a letter form. Blind removers break these texts; Puliora checks the neighbouring characters against Unicode script and emoji data first.

Where zero-width spaces break things

  • Code that fails to compile with “unexpected character” or an identifier that looks correct but is not.
  • JSON keys and CSV headers that do not match.
  • Searches, find-and-replace, spell checkers and word counts.
  • URLs, usernames and passwords pasted from a document.

Long runs of zero-width characters can also carry hidden data (zero-width steganography). Puliora flags them and tries to decode them — see the invisible text detector. To look at every character individually, use the Unicode inspector.

Frequently asked questions

What is a zero-width space?

U+200B is a character with no width. It tells software where a line may break, but it is invisible, so in copied text it usually just splits words for search and code.

How do I find zero-width spaces in Word or Google Docs?

They are invisible in most editors. Paste the text here: each one is listed with its line, and the Reveal view marks it in place. Then copy the cleaned text back.

Why does my code fail with an invisible character?

Compilers treat U+200B, U+FEFF or a bidi control as a real character. Developer mode shows the exact offset and code point.

Is removing ZWJ safe?

Not always: it is required inside emoji and in several scripts. Puliora removes it only where it has no function, and you can review each change.
