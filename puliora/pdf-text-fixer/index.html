PDF text fixer: copy from PDF without broken line breaks

Text copied from a PDF keeps a line break at the end of every line, splits words with hyphens and uses ligatures such as “ﬁ”. Document repair joins it back into clean paragraphs — and shows every fix.

Why PDF text breaks when you copy it

A PDF stores positioned lines, not paragraphs. When you copy, each visual line ends with a line break, hyphenated words stay split (“experi-ment”), and fonts may store “fi” or “fl” as a single ligature character. Encoding mistakes can add mojibake such as “â€™” instead of an apostrophe.

What Document repair does

  • Joins lines inside a paragraph; blank lines remain paragraph breaks; list items and headings stay on their own line.
  • Joins words hyphenated at the end of a line when the next line starts in lowercase.
  • Expands ligatures, collapses repeated spaces, trims line ends, keeps at most one empty line.
  • Repairs common encoding damage (UTF-8 read as Windows-1252).

Every repair is a separate change: open Changes and click one to undo it — useful for real compound words like “well-known”.

Can I upload the PDF itself?

Not at the moment. Extracting text from a PDF reliably (columns, tables, scanned pages) needs a full PDF engine, and we prefer not to claim support we cannot test properly. Copy the text from your PDF viewer and paste it here; the result is usually better than automatic extraction anyway.

Cleaning whole files? Use Files & batch. Odd spaces left over? See the non-breaking space remover.

Frequently asked questions

Why does text from a PDF have line breaks in every line?

PDFs store lines at fixed positions, not paragraphs, so each line ends with a break when copied.

Can I upload the PDF file?

No, PDF files are not supported; paste the copied text instead.

Will it join words split with hyphens correctly?

It joins them when the next line starts with a lowercase letter. Review real compound words in the Changes view and skip any change with one click.
